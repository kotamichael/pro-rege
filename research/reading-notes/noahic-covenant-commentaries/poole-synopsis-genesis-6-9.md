# Matthew Poole, *Synopsis Criticorum*, on Gen. 6:18, 8:20-22, and 9:9

**Full citation:** Matthew Poole, *Synopsis Criticorum Aliorumque S. Scripturae Interpretum* (London, 1669-76), vol. 1 (Genesis). ## What the source actually argues (as best determined)

The OCR is heavily degraded (17th-century long-s and ligature artifacts throughout), but three passages were located and read with reasonable confidence because their content correlates clearly with the known biblical text:

**On Gen. 6:18** ("*Ponam* [I will establish] my covenant with thee"), Poole catalogs **multiple distinct readings side by side**, in the format characteristic of the *Synopsis* — not asserting one as settled, but surveying the field:
- *Vel, Stabiliam pactum meum, nimirum Iridem* — "Or, 'I will make firm my covenant,' namely, the rainbow" — i.e., one cataloged reading takes the covenant named at 6:18 to refer forward to the rainbow sign given later in ch. 9.
- *Vel q.d. Pacifcar tecum tuam & tuorum incolumitatem; & a te vicissim stipulor ut Arcam fabrices, & ingrediaris, mihique fidas* — "Or, as if to say: 'I will make a pact concerning your safety and that of your household; and in turn I require of you that you build the ark, enter it, and trust me'" — a second reading centered on the immediate promise of physical preservation through the flood, conditioned on Noah's obedience.
- *Vel, fignificat pactum promiſſi ſeminis cum Noa firmatum, ut certo statuat Christum ex se nasciturum, & Deum in tanta ira relicturum seminarium Ecclesiae* — "Or, it signifies that the covenant of the promised seed was confirmed with Noah, so that he might be certain that Christ would be born from his line, and that God, even in such great wrath, would leave a seed-bed of the Church" — a third reading tying the Gen. 6:18 covenant directly to the Gen. 3:15 promised-seed covenant and its ultimate fulfillment in Christ.

**On Gen. 9:9** ("*I establish* [*heqim*] my covenant with you"), Poole's gloss is comparatively brief and direct: *Paratus sum ratum habere pactum quod inii vobiscum, ut servemini* — **"I am ready to hold as ratified the covenant which I [already] entered into with you, that you might be preserved."** The verb *inii* (perfect tense of *ineo*, "to enter into/undertake") explicitly casts the covenant as something already begun, not freshly instituted at this point — Poole reads *heqim* here as pointing backward to a prior covenant-act (naturally read as 6:18), now being ratified/held-as-binding, not as a fresh institution.

**On Gen. 8:21** (the "sweet savor" of Noah's sacrifice), the reading found emphasizes **acceptance of worship and gratitude** rather than substitutionary/expiatory language: *quietly... quiescere facientem iram Dei* ("tranquilizing... causing the wrath of God to grow quiet") and *Suscepit eam beneplacito oblationem... Placuit Deo* ("He received the offering with good pleasure... It pleased God"), explained further as *dictum more hominum, qui delectantur nidore assarum carnium* ("spoken after the manner of men, who delight in the smell of roasted meats") — and, most directly: *Prius factor impietatis offensas, nunc recreatur gratitudine Noae, & cultu suo* — "Whereas previously [God perceived] the stench of impious offenses, now he is refreshed by Noah's gratitude and his worship." No explicit expiation/substitution vocabulary (nothing corresponding to Murphy's "acknowledges his guilt... a substitute to satisfy this claim") was found in this specific passage — the emphasis here reads closer to worship-and-gratitude-accepted than to penal substitution.

## Bearing on the project's hypothesis and the Kline/VanDrunen critique

**A third, independent pre-critical witness for covenant unity, and the clearest direct hit yet on the *heqim* question.** Poole's gloss on 9:9 — "ready to hold as ratified the covenant which I [already] entered into with you" — is a clean, direct match for the reading Gentry/Wellum and Garris need (*heqim* = confirming a covenant already begun), and matches Murphy's "not merely makes... but ratifies" almost exactly, from an independent 1669 source. This is a real, substantive point *against* Leupold's own competing gloss (in the previous note in this folder), which read *heqim* at 6:18 as necessarily forward-looking ("must mean 'make' not 'keep,' for the covenant is not yet made"). Between Murphy and Poole both reading *heqim*-at-9:9 as backward-pointing ratification, and Leupold alone reading *heqim*-at-6:18 as necessarily prospective institution, the weight of this small sample now leans toward the unity/ratification reading — but this is three commentators, not a survey, and shouldn't be treated as settling a genuine lexical question that would need a proper Hebrew grammar/lexicon (BDB, HALOT) consulted directly, not inferred from commentators' paraphrases.

**The strongest single find: one of Poole's own cataloged readings of Gen. 6:18 explicitly ties Noah's covenant to the Gen. 3:15 promised-seed covenant**, "so that he might be certain that Christ would be born from his line" — independently corroborating Murphy's claim that the covenant with Noah was "really, though tacitly, formed with Adam in the garden," from a source nearly two centuries earlier. This is good, citable evidence — from the history of interpretation itself, not just from Murphy's own individual judgment — that reading Noah's covenant as a continuation of the one covenant of grace (this project's own manifesto thesis) has real precedent in 17th-century exegesis, not just among later commentators.

**The sacrifice question is now a genuine three-way split, not a two-way one.** Murphy: substitutionary/expiatory. Leupold: thanksgiving *and* propitiation blended. Poole (in the passage read): worship/gratitude accepted, no explicit substitutionary language. This project should NOT flatten this into "the tradition says X" against VanDrunen's "consecration, not expiation" claim — the honest picture is a real spread of emphasis among careful, conservative interpreters, with Murphy at one end and this Poole passage close to the other. VanDrunen's specific claim ("this sacrifice does not... mean that the Noahic covenant is redemptive rather than common") is still contestable — even a "gratitude and worship" reading doesn't obviously support treating Genesis 9 as a *religiously neutral, non-redemptive* arrangement — but the "propitiation" plank of the counter-argument shouldn't be asserted as if it were the uncontested historic consensus.
