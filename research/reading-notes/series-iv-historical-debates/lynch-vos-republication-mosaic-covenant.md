## What the source actually argues

Lynch expounds Geerhardus Vos's treatment of the Mosaic covenant in *Biblical Theology* (pp. 109-128) and concludes Vos's reading is confessionally orthodox — the Mosaic administration is grounded in the covenant of grace, not a meritorious republication of the covenant of works.

Vos's argument, per Lynch: the Mosaic covenant (Ex 24) is grounded on the prior redemption of the Exodus — "the exodus from Egypt *is* the Old Testament redemption" (Vos, 109) — so the law is given only *after* Israel already enjoys covenant blessings, which for Vos rules out a "Pharisaical philosophy" reading in which law-keeping is the meritorious ground of life-inheritance: "it is plain, then, that law-keeping did not figure at that juncture as the meritorious ground of life-inheritance. The latter is based on grace alone" (Vos, 127).

Vos does concede a real connection between Israel's obedience and retention of the land (typifying eternal life) — but insists this connection is not meritorious. Israel's theocracy typifies the heavenly kingdom, and the connection between obedience and blessing belongs "not to the legal sphere of merit, but to the symbolico-typical sphere of *appropriateness of expression*" (Vos, 127) — i.e., holiness is the "indispensable (though not meritorious) condition of receiving the inheritance" (Vos, 128), a condition Vos says Paul teaches for the new covenant too. As long as Israel "trusted and obeyed" corporately, the typology held; national apostasy (not imperfect law-keeping as such) is what triggers exile.

## Key quotes

> "It is plain, then, that law-keeping did not figure at that juncture as the meritorious ground of life-inheritance. The latter is based on grace alone, no less emphatically than Paul himself places salvation on that ground." (Vos, *Biblical Theology*, 127, quoted by Lynch)

> "[The connection between Israel's obedience and God's gifts] belongs not to the legal sphere of merit, but to the symbolico-typical sphere of appropriateness of expression." (Vos, 127)

> Lynch's own conclusion: "Vos's understanding of the Mosaic covenant is in line with confessional Reformed theology when he argues that the Mosaic administration and the law in particular, is grounded in the covenant of grace. The Mosaic economy is not a republication of the covenant of works where Israel merited the blessings or merited the continuation of the blessings."

## Bearing on the project's argument

This is directly useful for Series IV's historical lineage (Owen's administrative republication → Vos's non-meritorious "typal condition" reading → Kline's harder "substantial"/meritorious republication → Hodge/Murray disagreement → the modern OPC report). Vos sits closer to Owen/Ball than to Kline: he affirms a real, non-arbitrary connection between Israel's obedience and covenant blessing (so he's not simply Ball's "administration only" either), but explicitly and repeatedly denies that connection is *meritorious* — which is exactly the term Kline's "substantial republication" requires. Worth having Vos as an explicit middle term in the manifesto's historical-method paragraph, distinct from both extremes, rather than treating "the Klinean revival" as a simple, unbroken line from Owen forward.

Note this essay is about the covenant-of-works-at-Sinai/merit question specifically — it does not touch natural law or 2K political theology, so it belongs with Series IV material, not the natural-law-synthesis reading map's four strands.
