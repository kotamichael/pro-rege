# Contemporary interlocutors map

Working reference, not for publication — consolidates two rounds of author-verification research (2026-07-28) into one place. This is the raw material `00-foundations/bibliography.md` should eventually draw from. Confidence markers throughout: CONFIRMED (source text in hand), WELL-ATTESTED (consistent secondary description), UNVERIFIED (say so, don't guess).

## Pro-Kline / defenders of republication and 2K

- **R. Scott Clark** (Westminster Seminary California, Heidelblog) — CONFIRMED, the most substantial defender. Corpus clusters 2009-2017 (the original Merit and Moses/OPC-report period): "Seven Short Points on Republication," the Heidelcast republication series, a dedicated resource page at heidelblog.net/republication/ (blocked to automated fetch — check manually). No confirmed 2022-2026 piece directly answering Myers/Belcher/Garris found yet — worth a manual check of that resource page.
- **David VanDrunen** (WSC) — "Kline's former student," "the leading proponent of R2K" per Garris's chapter. *Natural Law and the Two Kingdoms* (2010), *Divine Covenants and Moral Order* (2014).
- **J. V. Fesko** (WSC) — *The Law Is Not of Faith* co-editor; *The Covenant of Works* (Oxford, 2020, historical-receptive); *Reforming Apologetics: Retrieving the Classic Reformed Approach to Defending the Faith* (Baker Academic, 2019) distinguishes "immediate" (unaffected) from "mediate" (noetically corrupted) natural-law principles — a genuinely useful conceptual tool for Strand 1 — and, confirmed 2026-07-29, argues at book length that Van Til's antithesis against natural theology rests on *Kantian*, not Reformed-scholastic, categories, and is historically false against Calvin/Turretin/Junius/the Westminster divines' own use of Thomistic proofs. Notable: Fesko is tracked elsewhere in this project as Escondido/2K-adjacent (WSC, *Law Is Not of Faith*), yet here he's a substantial *critic* of Van Til specifically on the analogy/natural-theology question — a reminder not to treat "pro-Kline" and "pro-Van-Til" as the same position by default. See Strand 4's new "Clark-Van Til controversy" subsection.
- **Michael Horton** (WSC) — moderated-Klinean; grace-administration with a real typological works-dimension.
- **Harrison Perkins** — *Reformed Covenant Theology* (Lexham, 2024), moderate/irenic Klinean systematic treatment.
- **Michael Beck** ("Two Age Sojourner" podcast/blog) — CONFIRMED, dissertation/monograph title now fully confirmed 2026-07-29: PhD, South African Theological Seminary, 2021, "A Dialectic Inquiry Concerning Meredith Kline's Covenant Theology as Architectonic Substructure of the Reformed Two-Kingdom Project," published in revised form as *Covenant Lord and Cultic Boundary: A Dialectic Inquiry Concerning Meredith Kline and the Reformed Two-Kingdom Project* (Pickwick, 2023), with Lee Irons involved in some capacity (foreword/contribution). This is **the one dissertation-level academic treatment of Kline's system found in the whole audit** — analyzes Kline against Theonomist, Perspectivalist, and Dooyeweerdian alternatives within the Reformed tradition. Read this directly, not just his podcast/blog material. Pastor, Gracenet Community Church, Wellington NZ; self-described "distinctly Klinean, Two-Kingdom and Baptist perspective." **Baptist, not paedobaptist** — note the confessional distance from this project's strict-1646-WCF frame when engaging him.
- **Lee Irons** (upper-register.com) — CONFIRMED, high-value primary source. Substantial paper corpus directly defending Kline against Merit and Moses, including "The Subservient Covenant: A 17th Century Precursor of Kline's View of the Mosaic Covenant" — Irons himself draws the Cameron/Bolton/Petto genealogical line this project's own Series IV already traces, just toward the opposite conclusion. Also has a paper titled **"Redefining Merit: An Examination of Medieval Presuppositions in Covenant Theology"** (via docslib.org mirror, per Andrew Smyth — see below) that reportedly *openly acknowledges* Kline's camp is redefining "merit" away from its historic scholastic sense rather than continuing it — an unusually honest, directly citable admission worth reading firsthand rather than relying on Smyth's characterization. Separately: was censured by OPC Presbytery of Southern California in 2003 over his teaching on the Decalogue/moral law's relationship to believers — a **distinct controversy**, six years before *The Law Is Not of Faith* (2009); don't conflate the two episodes.
- **Mark Karlberg** — named alongside Kline in a Puritan Board thread title ("In Defense of Moses: A Confessional Critique of Kline and Karlberg"); not yet independently researched — flag as a historical figure worth confirming (he has written extensively extending Kline's covenant theology in journal articles).
- **Brad Isbell** — CORRECTION to initial premise: not confirmed as a theological combatant on republication/2K. Ruling elder (PCA, Oak Ridge TN), Presbycast co-host, treasurer of Clark's Heidelberg Reformation Association — institutionally Escondido-adjacent but documented mainly as a PCA-politics/culture commentator, not a published defender of Kline's actual theology.

## Critics (several distinct angles — worth keeping them distinct, not treating "anti-Kline" as one position)

- **Stephen G. Myers**, *God to Us* (2021) — covenant-theology/exegetical angle; rejects Kline's law-covenant/promise-covenant distinction directly.
- **Richard Belcher Jr.**, *The Fulfillment of the Promises of God* (2020) — same angle, notably charitable tone (even a sympathetic Klinean reviewer conceded fair representation).
- **Ryan McGraw**, *What Is Covenant Theology?* (2013/2024) — same angle, popular level.
- **Stephen Wolfe**, *The Case for Christian Nationalism* (2022) — critiques VanDrunen specifically on the eschatology of the Adamic dominion mandate; himself a natural-law affirmer, rejects R2K's conclusion not its premise.
- **Zachary Garris** — ch. 6 of *Reformed Christian Politics* (Berith Press, 2026): confessional (WCF 19.4 general equity) + exegetical (Noahic covenant unity) + historical-theological critique. **Upgraded 2026-08-05: full chapter text extracted directly from the report PDF and read in full** (not just search summaries) — see `research/reading-notes/contemporary-kline-critics/garris-critique-of-modern-r2k.md` for the complete "Seven Arguments against the Klinean/R2K Division of the Noahic Covenant" and the natural-law section (VanDrunen's own definition of natural law already includes the First Table; Garris shows this being smuggled into a Second-Table-only civil jurisdiction via the Noahic covenant). Still the most directly quote-rich critique in this whole audit, now more so. Note: Garris is reportedly a controversial figure in some Reformed circles over unrelated complementarianism/gender-politics comments — engage his Kline-critique on its own merits, per `contemporary-interlocutor.md`'s fairness standard, without importing that separate controversy.
- **John Frame** — critiques the religious/secular-kingdoms *distinction* built on natural law, while explicitly agreeing with VanDrunen's actual exegesis of Rom 2:14-15.
- **Lane Tipton** (OPC republication study committee) — general Van Tilian anti-neutrality position on natural theology confirmed; his specific application to VanDrunen/Kline by name is still UNVERIFIED — a Puritan Board thread ("Any thoughts on Lane Tipton's interpretation of Kline in the OPC report?") and a Reformed Forum episode (reformedforum.org/ctc451/) are worth chasing directly.
- **Andrew Elam, Robert Van Kooten, Randall Berquist** — *Merit and Moses* (the original booklet that triggered the OPC study committee).
- **Michael Lynch** (Davenant Institute) — historical angle only (Vos's non-meritorious reading), not political theology. See `research/reading-notes/series-iv-historical-debates/lynch-vos-republication-mosaic-covenant.md`.
- **Matt Marino** (reformedclassicalist.com) — CONFIRMED as a critic too, not just a natural-theology-retrieval figure (correction to an earlier miss — see the reading map's Strand 1 entry for full detail). Directly engages R2K/VanDrunen ("Common Grace Inflated," "Who is the Sojourner?") and Van Til's role in the 1920s common-grace debate ("Common Grace Conflated"), plus dedicated apologetics pieces ("Van Til's Epistemology," "Noetic Effects of Sin" Objections) that overlap almost exactly with this project's own Strand 1 question. His angle: R2K's natural law is "selective," and defining "commonality" by what people actually share (rather than what God mandates) collapses into ethical pluralism — a real, independent version of this project's own suspicion of natural law's claimed neutrality. **Full reading-notes file now exists** (2026-08-05): `research/reading-notes/contemporary-kline-critics/marino-covenant-theology-video-critique.md`, synthesized from his "Covenant Theology" YouTube lecture series (considerably more developed than the blog posts — named citations of Frame's *Escondido Theology*, direct engagement with VanDrunen's own books, Fesko named directly as holding the same Sinai-republication view). His objective/subjective "common" distinction is functionally the same move as this project's own a1/a2 hypothesis split, arrived at independently. That same file now also covers the final 2 episodes (Rutherford on rule of law and resistance) of his "Foundations to Reformed Political Theory" playlist — genuinely useful Lex Rex material for Strand 3, but flagged there for a real register shift into unrelated contemporary political polemic (Muslim immigration) that should not be cited alongside the Lex Rex content itself. **A second, dedicated reading-notes file now also exists**: `research/reading-notes/contemporary-kline-critics/marino-natural-theology-discussions.md`, synthesizing his 8-episode "Discussions on Natural Theology" playlist — pure Strand 1 material (Romans 1-3 exegesis), no R2K/Kline engagement in any of the 8 episodes. Its most useful find: Marino explicitly defines natural law as a *species* of the *genus* natural theology, a sharper resolution of this project's own Strand 1 fusion question (see the reading map) than a flat "the categories fuse" reading, and he independently extends the a1/a2 hypothesis one level up (objective vs. subjective natural theology as such, not just natural law).
- **Jonathan Tomes** (postnuance.substack.com — reach it directly, poorly indexed by search) — CONFIRMED, corrected/expanded 2026-07-28 (round 4) after the project owner flagged a miss in the earlier pass. Currently the **most directly aligned contemporary voice found in this whole audit**: "The Twofold Kingdom of Christ" critiques VanDrunen's "spatial" (vs. properly "modal") reading of the two kingdoms, citing David Dickson against him, and independently treats the 1646-vs-1788 American revision as substantive — matching this project's own confessional discipline exactly, apparently without coordination with Wolfe/Garris/Myers/Belcher. "Good Works and Final Salvation in Reformed Orthodoxy" supplies a huge additional primary-source base (Turretin, Voetius, Bullinger, Davenant, Polanus, Ames, Owen) on the no-merit question, complementing Smyth's find. Full writeup: `research/reading-notes/contemporary-kline-critics/tomes-twofold-kingdom-and-good-works.md`. Worth reading his full ~32-post archive, not just these two pieces.
- **Andrew Smyth** ("Andrew's Myth" Substack, andrewsmyth.net/archive — not the bare root domain) — CONFIRMED, and now the richest single contemporary source found in this whole audit. Former WSCAL student turned critic. His central angle is genuinely distinct from all the above: **the definition of merit itself**, prior to any question about Sinai or 2K. Direct quote recovered from Kline's "Covenant Theology Under Attack": Kline holds Adam must have been capable of *strictly and properly meritorious* works in Eden (else Christ-as-second-Adam couldn't merit our salvation), and states in *Kingdom Prologue* (107-108) that denying this "stands condemned as subversive of that gospel." Smyth counters with Turretin (*IET* 17.5.6), Bavinck (*RD* 2.570), Calvin on Luke 17:7-10, and **WCF 7.1 and 16.5 themselves** — all denying strict human merit is possible even for unfallen Adam, on Creator/creature-distinction grounds, not merely sin. Smyth also states Kline wanted **WCF 7.1 revised** — checked directly 2026-07-29 and this specific claim doesn't hold up as stated (see `research/reading-notes/covenant-of-works-merit/README.md`); the confirmed textual-revision complaint is about ch. 19.4/23.3, not ch. 7. Full writeup: `research/reading-notes/contemporary-kline-critics/smyth-kline-merit-and-canon.md`. **Resolved 2026-07-29:** this merit/WCF-7.1 material now has its own dedicated tracking home, `research/reading-notes/covenant-of-works-merit/`, outside the four natural-law strands — it's prior to all of them (Series I's own subject matter), not a fifth strand.
- **Paul J. Barth** (purelypresbyterian.com) — establishmentarian/mediatorial-dominion angle, closest in spirit to this project's own natural-law hypothesis. Posted a citable Turretin quote in comments (*Institutes* III.xxii.iii) distinguishing the natural/essential kingdom from the economical/mediatorial kingdom — a real primary-source anchor for Strand 2/3. His own articles are confirmed on-topic (natural law, natural theology, Christ's mediatorial dominion, the establishment principle) but no dedicated Kline-specific piece found yet. **Live lead, 2026-07-29**: his article "Christ's Mediatorial Dominion and Two Kingdoms" explicitly draws on **John Brown of Haddington's** systematic theology to distinguish Christ's mediatorial kingship from his natural supremacy — since Brown's own corpus doesn't have an obviously-titled work on this specific question (see Strand 3), reading Barth's own citation directly is the fastest way to locate exactly where in Brown's writing this argument actually lives, rather than more blind searching through a 30-volume corpus.
- **"Reformed Libertarian" blog** — surfaced only via a Barth-article citation, described as critiquing VanDrunen's 2K formulation from its own angle; not yet independently researched.

## Academic secondary literature — added 2026-07-29, filling a real gap

Everything above this line was mostly popular/blog-tier engagement. This project also needs the actual academic literature, not summaries of it — audited 2026-07-29 after identifying this as a gap relative to genuinely rigorous historical-theological work.

- **Richard Muller** — the major living authority on Reformed scholasticism's own categories, previously absent from this whole project. Directly relevant: "Duplex Cognitio Dei in the Theology of Early Reformed Orthodoxy," *Sixteenth Century Journal* 10.2 (1979); "Reading Aquinas from a Reformed Perspective: A Review Essay," *Calvin Theological Journal* 53.2 (2018) — argues for *closer* Reformed-Thomist continuity than K. Scott Oliphint allows, directly testing this project's own "Reformed orthodoxy revised Aquinas" working assumption; "Was It Really Viral? Natural Theology in the Early Modern Reformed Tradition" (2018); *Post-Reformation Reformed Dogmatics* vol. 1, *Prolegomena to Theology* (Baker Academic, 2003 2nd ed.). Filed into Strand 1 of the reading map.
- **Seung-Joo Lee**, *The Orders of Nature and Grace: Thomistic Concepts in the Moral Thought of Franciscus Junius* (Brill, 2024, from a 2021 VU Amsterdam PhD) — "the first English monograph on Junius's theology in more than 40 years." Filed into Strand 2.
- **Stephen J. Grabill**, *Rediscovering the Natural Law in Reformed Theological Ethics* (Eerdmans, 2006) — draws on Calvin, Vermigli, Althusius, Turretin; not yet filed into a specific strand, worth doing so.
- **David VanDrunen**, *Natural Law and the Two Kingdoms* (Eerdmans, 2010) — his academic-register historical monograph, distinct from the popular *Living in God's Two Kingdoms* (Crossway, 2010, same year). This project has mostly engaged critiques of VanDrunen so far, not his own academic case directly — flagged in Strand 2.
- **Michael Beck**, *Covenant Lord and Cultic Boundary* (Pickwick, 2023) — see the corrected entry above; the one dissertation-level treatment of Kline's system that exists.
- **Joseph S. Moore**, *Founding Sins* (Oxford UP, 2016), and Robert Emery, "Church and State in the Early Republic: The Covenanters' Radical Critique," *Journal of Law and Religion* 25.2 (2009) — the closest adjacent academic scholarship to Strand 3 (American Covenanter mediatorial kingship), though neither is a doctrinal treatment of Symington/McLeod specifically. **That doctrinal treatment doesn't exist anywhere in the literature** — flagged in Strand 3 as a genuine opportunity for original contribution, not just a gap to work around.
- **Not found / confirmed absent:** a standalone academic dissertation *defending* Kline's republication view (as opposed to critiquing it or surveying it historically, as Fesko does) does not appear to exist in what's searchable — J.V. Fesko's *The Covenant of Works* (Oxford, 2020) remains the closest thing, and it's historical-receptive rather than a constructive defense.

## This project's own angle (for contrast)

Natural law's actual accessibility (given the noetic effects of sin) *and* its independence, or lack thereof, from Christ's mediatorial kingship — synthesizing Augustine/Aquinas/Turretin (Strand 1), Junius/Althusius/Calvin (Strand 2), Symington/McLeod (Strand 3), and Van Til (Strand 4). See `research/natural-law-synthesis-reading-map.md`. This is a genuinely distinct angle from all of the above critics — none of them combine 1646-specific citation, a full both-Testament natural-law argument, and direct political-theology engagement the way this project intends to.
